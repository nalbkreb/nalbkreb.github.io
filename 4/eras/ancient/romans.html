The Romans

Kingdom to Republic

Rome, mythically founded by Romulus and Remus, was originally a kingdom until the people grew tired of their monarchs and overthrew them, setting up a republic in its place. The republic quickly expanded beyond the Italian penensula and, after the defeat of Carthage, became the dominant power in the Mediterranian.

Republic to Empire

When the republic was around five hundred years old, a series of powerful military leaders, like Sulla and Caesar were named dictator for life. This displeased the Senate, and in Caesar's case, they took matters into their own hands, killing him and leading to a long civil war that ended with Caesar's nephew Octavian being proclamed Emperor. Thus began the empire.

Fall and Legacy

The empire was not a stable state, however, and suffered many poor rulers and schisms. The emperor Diocletian, fearing the empire had grown too large for one man to govern, split it in half. This worked well at first, but the two halfs grew apart and eventually the west fell under the rule of invading germanic kings. The empire lasted in the east for many centuries more and many have sinced tried to claim themselves as successors of Rome.
